TinkerPath — Product Brief

Understand it. Use it. Create with it.

A technology education platform for children and teenagers aged 7–16.

Live prototype: tinkerpath.onrender.com · Prepared November 2026 for the HNG presentation and for potential collaborators.

How to read this document. It describes both what exists today and what we plan to build. Anything that exists in the live prototype is marked Available now. Anything planned is marked Planned or listed under the MVP or future sections. TinkerPath is at an early stage: it has no users, partners or funding yet, and this document does not claim any.

1. Executive Summary

Children are growing up surrounded by technology and, increasingly, by artificial intelligence. Most of them learn to use apps and games, but few get a structured chance to understand how technology works, use it well, or create with it.

TinkerPath is an online technology education platform for ages 7–16. It brings together computer literacy, coding, artificial intelligence, creative technology and digital safety in one age-appropriate learning path. Lessons are short, visual and hands-on. A supervised AI companion helps children learn about AI safely, and parents see their child's progress in a dashboard.

A working prototype already exists, with a public website, parent sign-up, student accounts, 27 interactive sample lessons, and a parent dashboard. Over the next two weeks, as part of HNG, we plan to turn it into a focused MVP: strengthening the curriculum, adding a controlled AI learning companion, and making progress tracking reliable.

We are looking for developers, designers, educators and child-safety specialists to help build it.

2. Product Overview

Value proposition: TinkerPath gives children aged 7–16 a safe, structured path from curiosity to real technology skills, through short hands-on lessons, a supervised AI learning companion, and a parent dashboard that shows genuine progress.

TinkerPath is not just a coding school. Coding is one of five learning areas. The goal is broader: confident technology users, critical thinkers and future creators.

3. Problem Statement

  1. Children consume technology but rarely understand it. Most screen time is passive: watching, scrolling and playing. Few children learn how computers, the internet or AI actually work.
  2. AI is arriving before AI literacy. Children already meet AI in search, apps, games and chatbots. They are rarely taught that AI learns from examples, can be confidently wrong, and should not be given personal information.
  3. General-purpose AI tools are not designed for children. Independent reviewers such as Common Sense Media advise adult supervision for children using AI chatbots. Handing a child an unrestricted chatbot is not a learning plan.
  4. Existing options are fragmented. Families often have to choose between a coding class, a separate computer-skills course and generic online-safety advice. Few programmes connect these into one progression.
  5. Parents cannot see what their child is learning. Many parents are not technical themselves. Without clear progress information, it is hard to judge whether a programme is worth paying for.

5. Vision and Mission

Vision: A generation of young people who understand the technology shaping their lives, use it responsibly, and create with it.

Mission: To give every child aged 7–16 a safe, structured and enjoyable path from curiosity to practical technology skills, with parents involved every step of the way.

Principles

  • Every child belongs in tech. The platform is designed for all children, whatever their gender or background.
  • Learning by doing. Short explanations, then hands-on activities and projects.
  • AI literacy is basic literacy. Understanding AI, including its limits, is as important as using it.
  • Safety and privacy first. Collect as little as possible about children and keep parents in the loop.
  • Honesty. Clear prices, real progress data, and no promises we cannot keep.

Structure

Age path → Module → Lesson → Steps

  • Age paths: Explorers (7–9), Makers (10–12), Creators (13–16). Each path has its own lessons and examples. It is not one course stretched to fit every age.
  • Modules group 3–4 related lessons around one idea, for example "How AI learns".
  • Lessons take 10–15 minutes and mix a short explanation, an interactive activity, a quick check and, often, a mini project.
  • Next step: every lesson ends by pointing to the next one, so a learner never wonders what to do.

9. Student Experience

  1. Log in simply. A learner name (for example comet-482) and a 4-digit PIN set by the parent. No email, surname or photo.
  2. See where to start. The dashboard greets the learner by nickname and shows one large "Next up" card with the lesson title and time.
  3. Learn in short steps. Each lesson moves one step at a time with a progress bar: read, try, check, build.
  4. Get instant feedback. Activities explain why an answer is right or wrong and allow another try.
  5. Earn badges and build things. Badges for first lessons, completed modules and challenges such as "Fact checker"; mini projects saved to their profile.
  6. Always know what's next. A completion screen shows the score, any new badge, and the next lesson.

Example activities by age (available now):

  • 7–9: guide a kite through a maze using Forward and Turn commands, then debug the program.
  • 10–12: train a tiny AI to sort suns from leaves, then discover why it fails on shapes it never saw (an introduction to bias).
  • 13–16: write HTML and CSS in a live editor and see the page update instantly.

10. Parent Experience

  1. Quick onboarding. Five questions about the child (age group, interests, experience, goals, nickname), then a recommended path with the reasons shown.
  2. Consent and control. The parent creates the account, gives consent, and sets the child's PIN.
  3. Clear progress. Lessons completed, quiz scores, projects, badges and last active date.
  4. Understanding, not just numbers. A "What your child is learning" view explains each module and why it matters, in plain language.
  5. AI visibility. Every prompt the child writes in the AI Lab, including any blocked by the safety filters, appears in the parent's dashboard.
  6. Account management. Plan and enrolment status, cancellation, email preferences and a support form.

11. AI Learning Companion and Child Safety

Approach

Children learn how AI works before they use it. They train a model, see how missing examples cause bias, and practise spotting AI mistakes. Only then do older learners use a live AI, and only inside a controlled environment.

Available now: AI Lab (practice mode)

  • Prompt-writing missions with instant feedback on four skills: task, audience, format and detail.
  • Example replies are pre-written, chosen by how clear the prompt is. No live AI service is connected, and the interface says so.
  • Safety filters already check every prompt before it is accepted.
  • A guided lesson helper offers pre-written hints and simple explanations. Children choose from buttons and cannot type into it.

Planned for the MVP: AI learning companion

A helper inside lessons that can explain an idea again, give a hint, or answer a question about the current topic. Without a key, it falls back to guided practice mode.

SafeguardHow it works
Age-based accessAges 7–9: guided, offline AI activities only. Ages 10+: live companion only if a parent switches it on.
Server-side onlyThe AI service is called from our server. No AI keys in the browser, and no direct access to general-purpose chatbots.
Personal-information filterMessages containing names, addresses, schools, phone numbers, emails or passwords are blocked before they reach the AI.
Topic filters, both waysPrompts and replies are checked for unsuitable topics. Worrying messages show supportive guidance and are flagged to the parent.
Constrained instructionsThe AI is told to keep answers short and school-appropriate, stay on the lesson, avoid acting as a friend or companion, and not do homework for the child.
Daily limitsA cap on live AI messages per child per day.
Full parent visibilityEvery message and reply is logged in the parent dashboard.
Human reviewA process for reviewing flagged conversations and improving the filters.

Our honest position: these safeguards reduce risk; they cannot remove it entirely. Filters can miss things and AI can make mistakes. That is why live AI is off by default, limited to older learners, and always visible to parents.

Child data protection

  • Minimal data: a nickname, age group, interests and learning progress. No surname, date of birth, school, photo or child email.
  • Parent consent: only a parent or guardian can create an account, in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, which requires parental consent for processing children's data. Policies need legal review before launch.
  • No child-to-child messaging.

12. What Makes TinkerPath Different

  1. Broader than coding. Computer literacy, coding, AI, creative technology and digital safety in one connected path.
  2. AI literacy built in, safely. Children learn how AI works and where it fails, in a supervised environment, not an open chatbot.
  3. Designed per age group. Separate paths and examples for 7–9, 10–12 and 13–16.
  4. Parents see real learning. Progress, projects and every AI interaction, explained in plain language.
  5. Privacy by design. No child email or identifying details; parent-controlled accounts.
  6. Short, hands-on lessons. 10–15 minutes, built around doing rather than reading.
  7. Local relevance. Examples and pricing designed with Nigerian families in mind, while working for learners anywhere.
  8. Honest by default. No inflated claims, clear demo labels, and transparent limitations.

15. Suggested Technical Architecture

Current prototype — Available now

  • Server: Node.js with no external libraries; pages built on the server.
  • Database: SQLite (file-based).
  • Frontend: HTML, CSS and plain JavaScript; responsive design.
  • Hosting: Render (free plan). Demo data resets when the server restarts.
  • Integrations built but not active: Paystack payments, Resend email, an AI provider for live AI.

The prototype is ideal for demonstrating the product. For a team of several developers working in parallel, we suggest the structure below, reusing the prototype's curriculum content, lesson activity designs and visual identity.

LayerSuggestionWhy

AI safety layer (request flow)

  → eligibility check (age group, parent opt-in, daily limit)

17. Expected Impact

These are the outcomes we aim for and intend to measure. They are goals, not results.

For children

  • Understand how computers, the internet and AI work, in their own words.
  • Build real things: programs, web pages and projects.
  • Think critically about technology, including checking what AI tells them.
  • Stay safe and protect their privacy online.

For parents

  • Clear insight into what their child is learning and how they are progressing.
  • Confidence that their child is using AI in a supervised, age-appropriate way.

For the wider community

  • More children, of all genders, exploring technology early.
  • A pipeline of young people better prepared for a technology-driven future.

How we will measure it (planned): lesson completion rates, quiz improvement over time, projects completed, parent feedback, and AI safety incident reviews.
